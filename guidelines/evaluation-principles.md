# Evaluation Principles

These principles guide future reviews. They define a method; they are not findings about any output.

## Prompt first, output second
Read the full prompt before viewing or listening. Identify the requested content and constraints. Assess prompt adherence separately from general quality so polish does not hide a mismatch.

## Evidence-specific descriptions
Describe what is observable and where it occurs: image region, video timestamp/frame range, or spoken phrase/time range. Explain which criterion or prompt constraint the evidence bears on. Avoid unsupported labels such as “bad,” “weird,” or “unnatural.” Record ambiguity and uncertainty.

## Objective versus subjective judgments
Mark verifiable facts (for example, whether a requested object is present) separately from interpretive judgments (for example, whether a style is appealing). State the basis for a subjective judgment and do not present personal preference as an objective defect.

## Consistent scoring
Use the rubric anchors consistently. Score prompt adherence and quality separately. Use “uncertain” or “not applicable” when justified; do not force a score. Apply the same standard across outputs and record any deviation.

## Fatigue and mood
Work in manageable sessions. Do not rate while tired, rushed, distracted, or noticeably affected by mood. Pause and resume when able to give items comparable attention. Do not let earlier outputs, model expectations, or a desired conclusion influence a current item.

## Listening conditions and breaks
Review audio in a quiet environment at a comfortable, consistent volume. Log interruptions, background noise, playback problems, and other relevant conditions. Do not attribute a limitation of the listening setup to the audio. Take a 30-minute break between extended blocks; stop earlier if attention or consistency declines.

## Ownership and integrity
The project owner performs all actual reviews and authors all ratings, observations, comparisons, and rationales. Never fabricate results, counts, flaw descriptions, winners, or justifications. Preserve the distinction between evidence, interpretation, and conclusion.
